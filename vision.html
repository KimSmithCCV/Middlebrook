Our Vision

It all starts with land.

Middlebrook is Iowa's first Agrihood: a community in Cumming built around a working farm, where neighborhoods grow alongside the land instead of replacing it.

From Our Founder

“I envision a different future where development and agriculture unite to build a better community.”

A patchwork of disconnected sprawl has emerged at the outskirts of every metro, as growth and development pressures drive a wedge between farming families and the generational asset of the landscape they call home.

People connect with agriculture from different directions and perspectives, but they share a common appreciation for the land, how food is grown, and where it comes from.

Middlebrook brings development and agriculture together. Neighborhoods, parks, streets, and local businesses are stitched together by the very agricultural landscape that would otherwise be erased, creating a community for all to enjoy.

More than a neighborhood

An Agrihood is a master-planned community built around a working farm or town garden rather than traditional suburban amenities. Instead of development pushing agriculture further away from population centers, it preserves land for farming and gardening where people live.

Middlebrook is built with health and wellness, sustainability, and community connection at its core. At the center sits a two-acre town garden with a self-service Farm Stand, a you-pick flower field, community garden plots, two all-season high tunnels, and a flock of free-range chickens, all within a 15-minute walk of every home and business.

Visit the town garden

Restored, not replaced

We kept what was already here

An Agrihood keeps farming close to home. Middlebrook also kept the buildings that came before it. Instead of clearing them away, we restored each one and gave it a new role in the life of the community.

The plan

Planned with the land, not over it

Middlebrook is a vibrant network of neighborhoods for families of all ages that nourish community through healthy food, farming, lively local events, and diversified local businesses, creating deep-rooted pride and attachment to the land. Each of the tranquil residential neighborhoods has access to agricultural enclaves, natural parks, and trail connectivity, as well as the convenience of a pedestrian-focused town center.

The plan follows the idea at the heart of landscape architect Ian McHarg’s Design with Nature: let the land lead. Neighborhoods sit on the higher ground. Natural drainage becomes greenways and ponds. Existing tree rows and heritage trees stay where they are, and the Great Western Trail runs through the middle of it all.

Beliefs in action

The ideas behind every street

  1. 01

    Walkability at the core

    Every Middlebrook business and amenity is within a 15-minute walk of the centrally located town garden. Wide sidewalks and bike paths connect the neighborhoods, and the Great Western Bike Trail runs through the center of Middlebrook, so every home has close access to it.

  2. 02

    Where neighbors say hello

    Neighborhood grill outs, spontaneous happy hours, and Agrihood get-togethers happen often, and the event calendar on the Farm Grounds runs from spring through fall.

  3. 03

    Front porches face the street

    Homes draw on the farmhouse forms of the region, with modern, transitional, and traditional styles side by side. Front porches and pitched rooflines give every street a common character.

  4. 04

    Streets made for people, not garage doors

    In most new neighborhoods, the garage door is the first thing you see. Middlebrook's design guidelines keep garages from dominating the streetscape, so the street is lined with front porches, front doors, and windows instead. It is a big part of why the neighborhoods feel walkable, and why neighbors see and greet each other every day.

  5. 05

    A mix of homes on the same streets

    Lofts, cottages, townhomes, villas, lots, and single-family homes share the same neighborhoods, at a range of sizes and price points, so residents can stay rooted as their needs change.

  6. 06

    Built with geothermal and solar

    Geothermal heating and cooling serves Town Center, the Homestead Townhomes, the Cottages, and the Lofts, and is offered as an option in every new build. Town Center commercial buildings use solar power, and locally sourced building material is used throughout the Agrihood.

  7. 07

    An extension of Cumming

    Town Center grows from historic downtown Cumming instead of competing with it, with local shops, dining, and services, and a second phase of storefronts and lofts coming in 2027.

Our roots

Built on the history of Cumming

The town of Cumming grew up along the railroad in the late 1800s, and Middlebrook grows from that same ground.

  1. 1888

    The town of Cumming is platted on October 26, with 24 lots across two blocks along the railroad line.

  2. 1900

    Cumming’s schoolhouse is built. Its most famous student is former Iowa Senator Tom Harkin. Today it is home to Middlebrook Mercantile.

  3. 1992

    The old Chicago Great Western rail line becomes the Great Western Trail, connecting Des Moines south to Martensdale through the heart of Cumming.

  4. 2018

    The Farm at Middlebrook is founded, and Iowa’s first Agrihood takes root.

  5. 2019

    The first homes arrive at Great Western Crossing, a historic timberframe barn is relocated to The Farm, and Fridays at the Farm begins.

  6. 2021

    Great Western Crossing hosts the Greater Des Moines Home Builders Association Home Show Expo, and Mayor Tom Becker Park opens: an agriculture-inspired playground complete with a play barn, a windmill to climb, and a tractor to drive.

  7. Today

    About 80 homes, the Lofts, a growing Town Center, Wilson’s Orchard and Farm, the 50-acre Middlebrook Meadow, and a full season of events on the Farm Grounds. The Cottages open in fall 2026.
